# CIDM 6395 Capstone Project Proposal

## EVShift: An Analysis of the U.S. Automotive Industry's Transition Toward Electrification

### Project Overview
The proposed capstone project, **EVShift**, will examine the transition of the U.S. automotive industry from conventional gasoline-powered vehicles toward hybrid electric vehicles (HEVs), plug-in hybrid electric vehicles (PHEVs), and battery electric vehicles (BEVs) over the past 15 years. Rather than beginning with a predetermined software product, the project will begin with historical automotive and energy data from publicly available sources such as the U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA), U.S. Department of Energy (DOE), Alternative Fuels Data Center (AFDC), and U.S. Energy Information Administration (EIA). The objective is to identify meaningful trends, relationships, and potential problem areas within the industry's transition toward electrification. Findings from the data analysis will then guide the development of a software-based solution. The project will integrate competencies in Data Management (DM), Business Analytics (BA), Software Systems (SS), and Cybersecurity and Networking (CN).

### Data Management (DM)
The Data Management component will focus on acquiring, evaluating, cleaning, transforming, integrating, and storing historical automotive and energy data from multiple public sources. Potential data elements include model year, manufacturer, vehicle and fuel type, fuel economy, vehicle technology, electric vehicle registrations, geographic location, and historical gasoline prices. Relevant data will be modeled using an Entity-Relationship Diagram (ERD) and organized within a relational database. SQL will be used to create and populate the database and retrieve information through joins, aggregation, subqueries, and other relational database operations. This component will establish a structured and reliable data foundation for subsequent analysis.

### Business Analytics (BA)
The Business Analytics component will investigate how vehicle electrification has evolved over time and identify factors associated with changes in adoption. Analysis may examine the growth of HEVs, PHEVs, and BEVs; differences among automotive manufacturers; geographic differences in electric vehicle adoption; changes in fuel economy and vehicle technology; and relationships between historical gasoline prices and electrified vehicle adoption. Statistical methods and data visualization techniques will be used for exploratory analysis, trend analysis, manufacturer comparisons, correlation and/or regression analysis, and, where supported by the data, forecasting. The analysis will be used to identify a meaningful business, consumer, or information problem that can be addressed through software development.

### Software Systems (SS)
The Software Systems component will be developed in response to the problem or information gap identified during the analytical phase. Rather than defining the final application in advance, the results of the Data Management and Business Analytics components will guide its requirements and functionality. The resulting solution is expected to be a web-based decision-support or information system that allows users to interact with relevant automotive data, analytical findings, comparisons, or predictive information. Potential functionality may include interactive dashboards, manufacturer or state comparisons, historical trend exploration, cost comparisons, or forecasting tools. The application will integrate a web-based user interface, application logic, and the project's underlying data resources.

### Cybersecurity and Networking (CN)
The Cybersecurity and Networking component will focus on protecting the resulting application and its supporting infrastructure. Appropriate controls may include secure application configuration, input validation, secure database queries, authentication and authorization where applicable, protection of credentials and configuration information, HTTPS/TLS concepts, database access controls, logging, and secure network architecture. A network/security architecture diagram and selected security testing will be used to document and evaluate how the completed system and its data can be appropriately protected.

### Synthesis and Final Artifact
The final synthesis artifact will integrate the project's data, analytical findings, software solution, and security considerations into a cohesive information system. The project will follow a data-first progression in which historical public data is acquired and organized, analyzed to identify patterns and potential problems, used to inform the design of a software solution, and secured using appropriate cybersecurity and networking practices. All datasets, database artifacts, analytical work, source code, documentation, security artifacts, and execution instructions will be maintained within a single GitHub repository.
